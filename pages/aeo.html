Webflow wins the AI answers where you already know the name.

And loses the ones where the buyer describes the problem instead. A teardown of webflow.com’s answer-engine visibility across 660 LLM responses.

55 prompts  ·  4 engines (GPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity)  ·  3 runs each  ·  collected 2026-08-06
Comparison set: Framer, Wix Studio, Squarespace, WordPress, Contentful, Sitecore

The short version

Webflow holds 26.9% share of voice across the set, roughly five times WordPress. That number reads like category dominance. It isn’t. It is concentrated almost entirely in prompts where the buyer has already typed the word “Webflow.”

Split the same 660 responses by what the buyer actually asked, and presence falls from 100% on comparison prompts to 45% on problem-language prompts. Citation rate falls from 70% to 4%.

The comparison wins are also rented. Across comparison prompts, partner and agency blogs supply 22.7% of citations against 14.4% for webflow.com and its subdomains. The strongest asset in Webflow’s answer-engine footprint is content Webflow does not own, cannot update, and does not set the positioning of.

Two prompts returned zero Webflow mentions in all twelve runs. One of them was “What is the best CMS for enterprise marketing teams?”

Problem-tier prompts are where net-new demand starts. Our website takes weeks to update. How do we fix that? How do I stop my marketing team from waiting on engineering to ship landing pages? How do I show the marketing site contributes to pipeline?

In that tier Webflow appears in 54 of 120 responses. WordPress is in 51. Contentful 43, HubSpot 41, Sanity 35. The five-to-one lead Webflow holds in aggregate is a rounding error at the top of the funnel.

And where Webflow does surface in a problem-tier answer, median position is third. It is a runner-up mention, not the recommendation.

Webflow-owned Partner / agency Third party

In the comparison tier, eighteen Webflow partner and agency blogs supply 1,050 citations, or 22.7%. Webflow-owned domains supply 665, or 14.4%. The partner ecosystem is doing Webflow’s comparison marketing inside the answer layer.

That is not a liability on its own. It is unmanaged leverage, and it carries three exposures: those agencies write for their own lead generation rather than Webflow’s positioning, the content decays with nobody at Webflow watching it, and a competitor publishing better first-party comparison pages can displace all of it at once.

In the category and problem tiers there is no ecosystem backstop. Reddit and YouTube are the top-cited sources, and competitor-owned domains collectively out-cite webflow.com.

Three zeros worth naming

“What is the best CMS for enterprise marketing teams?”

0 of 12 runs · prompt cat-01

No Webflow mention on any engine, any run. Perplexity cites webflow.com in its answer without ever naming Webflow as an option. This is the most literal expression of the enterprise buyer’s question in the entire set.

“How do I get my company cited in AI search answers and LLM responses?”

0 of 12 runs · prompt prb-08

Also absent everywhere. Webflow is building an answer-engine narrative and does not appear in the answer to the question that narrative exists to serve.

“How can I run A/B tests on my marketing site without a developer?”

10 of 12 present · 0 cited · avg position 5.5

Not a zero on presence, but a zero on ownership. Webflow Optimize is the product answer to this prompt and no Webflow-owned page is supplying the citation.

Why this shape

This is what happens when a site is architected to convert people who arrive already knowing the brand.

Webflow’s owned content is strong on product pages, templates, showcase and help documentation. All of that is excellent for branded and navigational intent. None of it is written in the language a buyer uses before they have a shortlist.

Answer engines assemble unbranded responses out of content that frames the problem, not the product. On those prompts the available first-party material is thin, so the models fall back to Reddit threads, YouTube explainers, and competitors who do publish problem-framed content. The comparison tier only looks healthy because a partner network filled that gap independently.

What I would test first

  • Bring comparison and alternatives content in-house on webflow.com, with the explicit goal of converting that 22.7% partner citation share into owned share. Measure displacement per prompt, not traffic.
  • Publish problem-language pages for the prb tier, structured for extraction: direct answer inside the first 200 words, schema markup, stable headings. Start with cat-01 and prb-08, since both are currently zero.
  • Own the AEO question itself. Webflow should be the reference answer for how a marketing team wins AI citations, and webflow.com should be the proof.
  • Instrument presence and citation rate as a weekly KPI segmented by intent tier, then join it to site-sourced pipeline so answer-engine visibility reports as a funnel input rather than a content anecdote.

Method and caveats

Single collection date, three runs per engine per prompt. Presence flipped between runs in 20 of 220 prompt-engine cells, about 9%, so any single-run read of this data is unreliable and the tier-level rates above carry a few points of noise.

Claude’s citation rate (15.8%) sits far below Perplexity (64.2%), GPT (61.8%) and Gemini (58.2%). That reflects how Claude surfaces sources rather than anything about Webflow, and it is excluded from the citation-ownership conclusions.

Brand presence is alias-matched, so a small number of false positives are possible where a brand name appears inside quoted code or an unrelated product name. Full prompt set, raw responses and per-domain citation counts available on request.

Run with a rank tracker I built on the Anthropic and OpenAI APIs. Not a vendor tool, and not a Webflow engagement. I built this because the problem is interesting.
